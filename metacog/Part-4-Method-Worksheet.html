Part 4 — The Method (your “fishing pole”)

HRB794 · A worksheet for reflecting and building. It is not a template to fill in for points, and there is no answer key. It only asks the questions; you supply the method.

Parts 1–3 are the fish. Part 4 is the pole — the account of how your team built Parts 1–3 and how the Herbshed keeps updating them after you’re gone. It is the one part whose value doesn’t decay when a price or a regulation changes.

Work the modules against one real decision your team actually made. Answer from what you did, not from how it “should” be done. When finished, use Print / Save as PDF at the bottom.

What this is looked at for (there is no external key)

  • Derived, not generic. It reads like an account of what your team actually did, not a textbook process that would fit any project.
  • Calibrated. Your stated confidence matches your evidence; it says plainly what it does not cover.
  • Reusable. A Herbshed member could run it next year, on a tool or herb you never touched, without you in the room.
  • Honest about change. It shows where your thinking was wrong or incomplete and what moved it.
Module 1

Reconstruct the actual process

Take one question Fox Haven actually put to your team. Reconstruct the real path from their words to your answer.

Module 2

Your decision structure

How your matrix or heuristic actually decides — before you tidy it up.

Module 3

Make it reusable (the no-author test)

For each thing your team built — matrix, heuristic, guide — put it through the same three questions.

Module 4

The transfer: culture, process, framing

One concrete change Fox Haven could adopt in each, so they answer future questions without you.

Module 5

Generalize (strip out the herbs)

If the method only works on this project, it isn’t a method yet.

Module 6

What changed — in you, and in the group

Six weeks ago the group could not shepherd a GRAS designation; now you can describe the pathway. Trace one such shift.

Module 7 — optional

Critique these fragments

Three sample “Part 4” fragments. None is a model answer; each fails in a specific way. Say what is wrong, and what you would need to see instead.
