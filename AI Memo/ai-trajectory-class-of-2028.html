02 · The price of thought

Thought is getting cheaper faster than anything before it.

Epoch AI, September 2026: the price of a fixed level of AI capability has fallen 47% per quarter, 13x per year, since 2023. Newly released frontier capability falls 75x per year; two years after release it still falls 4.7x. In plain terms, whatever the best model does today costs about a tenth as much a year from now. Example: a 75% score on GPQA Diamond cost $0.30 a question in January 2025 and $0.0004 in July 2026.

03 · Capability

How much better the models get by 2028.

Epoch AI, April 2026: capability growth sped up when reasoning models arrived in late 2024, a jump plus a 2 to 3x steeper trend. The frontier has gained 14 ECI points a year since, against 6 before. The length of task a model can finish on its own has doubled every 89 days since 2024, against 196 over the long run. One research group fits a curve that levels off from mid-2026; through September the data has not leveled off, and METR's test can no longer measure the top models. Three forecasts below. I hold the first.

05 · The stance

The one control that changes what students learn from it.

The randomized trial we have: Bastani and colleagues, PNAS 2025, about a thousand high-school mathematics students. Unguided GPT-4 access raised practice scores 48% and lowered the unassisted exam 17%. A guarded tutor on the same model raised practice 127% with no exam penalty. Students in the unguided arm, in the authors' words, "most often simply ask for the answer."

06 · Where it moves first

Automation follows verification. So does grading.

The speed-up on slide 03 happened where a right answer can be checked automatically, because that is what the training rewards. That gradient is a map of the curriculum. Checkable material is already handled at the cheapest tier. Judged material sits on the slower slope. The line moves right every year as training learns to grade with rubrics.

08 · Settled

Questions the evidence has closed.

Do students use it?

95% of UK undergraduates use AI and 94% use it on assessed work. In the US, 57% use it in coursework at least weekly and one in five daily. Use is the baseline condition of the classroom, whatever the syllabus says.

HEPI 2026, n=1,054 · Gallup 2026, n=3,801

Is access the equity barrier?

The price of a fixed level of capability falls 13x a year. The best open-weight models trail the closed frontier by four months and can be run by anyone. Every major lab offers a free student tier. Within a degree window, cost stops being the thing that separates students.

Epoch AI, Sep 2026 and May 2026

Does waiting pay?

Even the levels-off case freezes the frontier above 16-hour unattended tasks and 91% on PhD-level science, at near-zero cost. There is no later point at which the decision gets easier or the information gets better.

METR, May 2026 · OpenRouter pricing, Jul 2026

Is detection a strategy?

At $0.0004 a question, rewriting a draft until it passes is free. Direct submission of generated text rose from 3% to 12% in two years, most of it under detection and prohibition regimes. Detection selects for concealment, which is the condition that harms learning.

Epoch AI 2026 · HEPI 2024 to 2026

Does unguided use harm learning?

Yes. In the one randomized trial of size, students with unrestricted access scored 17% lower on the unassisted exam. Students with a guarded tutor built on the same model showed no penalty and larger practice gains. The variable that mattered was guidance, and guidance is something a program decides to provide.

Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı & Mariman, PNAS 2025

09 · Open work

Questions still open, and the work they name.

What does a valid assessment look like once the checkable half is automated?

Training rewards what can be checked automatically, so the assessments cheapest for us to grade are the ones models master first. What survives is the expensive kind: judgment under uncertainty, reasoning the student has to defend, work observed while it is being done. Building that instrument, calibrating raters on its middle anchors, and paying for the design are all unresolved.

Epoch AI, Apr 2026 (speed-up concentrated where answers are checkable) · HEPI 2026 (12% direct submission)

How is an output checked when no automatic checker exists?

In checkable domains the model's own training loop does the verifying. Everywhere else a person has to judge, and judgment is slow, inconsistent between readers, and weakens as outputs become more fluent: the better a passage reads, the less of it gets checked. The open problem is a method of verification that scales with the volume of output without collapsing into trust. Candidates include an independent second reader (human or model), breaking a claim into parts that can each be checked, sampled audits, and calibrating readers against one another. None is settled, in this field or any other.

Epoch AI, Apr 2026 (no speed-up where checking is hard) · METR 2026 (performance falls when work is judged as a whole rather than by tests)

When access is free, what does equity require?

The price trend removes cost as a barrier inside the degree window. What stays scarce is guided practice: a person who shows the use, corrects it, and sets the friction so that the easy path is the learning path. Students who do not get that at home get it only from us. The open question is how to provide it across a faculty with uneven fluency, at scale, without asking the students who most need it to announce that they need it.

HEPI 2026: 36% feel encouraged, 38% are given tools, 48% say staff help build the skill · Gallup 2026: 53% of institutions discourage or prohibit, 57% of students use weekly

Can the RCT's guided condition be reproduced in a graduate health program?

The RCT removed the exam penalty by changing the tool, not the student: a tutor that would not hand over the answer. Our students arrive habituated to the tool that will. Three questions follow. Do guardrails built into course tooling transfer from high-school mathematics to case reasoning and clinical judgment? Will a student who already owns the unguided tool use the guided one? And what does the guided condition look like when the task has no single right answer to withhold?

Bastani et al., PNAS 2025: practice +48% unguided, +127% guided; exam −17% unguided, no penalty guided

Does the forecast depend on the models becoming minds?

No. Everything on page 01 was done by systems that, on most accounts, still lack a lasting sense of self, attention driven by their own goals, anything like feeling, and continuity from one session to the next. The projections in this memo need none of that: they need the price and capability trends to hold, and the conservative case needs only what has already shipped. Separately, a growing number of researchers in cognitive science and machine learning now treat those missing pieces as questions that can be studied and answered, with candidate designs on the table, rather than as permanent mysteries. If they are right, the range on page 03 is a floor.

Bach, 2024 to 2026 talks (what current systems lack) · Epoch and METR trends require no assumption about machine minds

10 · My conclusion

Plan for the top of the range.

My projection is the maximal case: by the class of 2028, students carry systems that complete weeks of unattended work per task, at a semester cost below one textbook, and no vendor can withhold them. The conservative floor on this page asks the same of us. The one variable the school controls is whether the use is taught.
