# Energetics Atlas: notes for instructors

The atlas is for students who already work in one energetic system. It lets them lay that system onto five others and see what each one can and cannot read. These notes are for teachers who want to use it. They are kept off the student page, so the page can show rather than tell.

The page is at <https://johncourie.github.io/pedagogy-tools/reference/energetics-atlas/>.

## What a landing means

A point lands on another map's point when the two share a quality, as the page assigns it from the cited sources. The Wood phase lands on Vata because Maciocia (2015) gives wood the climate wind and Wood (2004, ch. 1) sets vata beside wind. A landing is a shared word in two legends. It is not an equivalence, and the atlas never claims one.

## Where it can be read too strongly

- **As a crosswalk.** The tempting move is to chain landings into identities: vata is air is wind is constriction, so a vata person needs relaxants. Each link holds inside its own source. The chain holds nowhere. When students produce chains, ask which author makes each link, and whether any one author makes them all.
- **As a typology.** The doshas and the somatotypes invite people to type themselves. The doshas read a constitution and its disturbance. The somatotypes only describe present shape. Neither is an identity. The link between them is one Western reading that Wood (2004, ch. 7) reports, and Valiathan (2003, p. 55) complicates it: Caraka's constitution turns more on the digestive fires than on the doshas.
- **As a prescriber.** The person and the six herbs show how a declared lexicon of invented herbs moves a reading on three axes: toward balance, across it, deeper into the pattern, or nowhere. That is the logic of matching by contraries. It does not select or dose a real herb.

## Where it shows too little

The atlas compares legends. That is the formal layer, the matching, and it is where the systems look most alike, because any of them can be written as a table of qualities. The differences that matter sit in two layers the page cannot draw: how each system is read, and what it is built to decide.

| Map | What it reads | Grain and timescale | Read with | Built to decide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Four qualities | A quality of a substance or a state, by degree | Intensity more than duration | Effects on the body and senses: "it was more objective to define the four qualities by their effects" (Wood, 2004, ch. 2) | Which remedy, at what degree, against which excess |
| Tissue states | How one tissue is behaving: activity, tone, nourishment | One tissue. Days to years, with chronic cases settling into atrophy, torpor or depression (Wood, 2004, ch. 3) | Hands, tongue, pulse, discharges | Which class of remedy (Wood, 2004, Part Two) |
| Five phases | Relations among organ networks | Cycles: seasons and stages of development (Maciocia, 2015, Table 2.1), and the passage of a disorder between networks | Pulse, tongue, complexion, the sequence of symptoms and emotions | Where to act, often away from the symptom: "tonify the mother organ" (Yang, 2010) |
| Eight principles | A disorder: where it sits, its nature, and the strength of the pathogen against the body's resistance | The course of an illness, exterior to interior, full to empty | Chinese diagnosis, summarized into four pairs (Maciocia, 2015, p. 444) | The strategy: expel or tonify, release the exterior or treat the interior |
| Doshas | A constitution and its disturbance | A lifelong baseline and a current state | Digestion, elimination, sleep, temperament, with the digestive fires at the center for Caraka (Valiathan, 2003, p. 55) | Diet, daily regimen, and a substance's taste, quality, potency and post-digestive effect (Department of AYUSH, *Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India*) |
| Somatotypes | The body's present shape | Slow: "a description of present morphological conformation" (Heath & Carter, 1967, p. 70) | Measurement from the outside | Nothing therapeutic |

Each system is calibrated to a grain and a timescale. Each is read through an instrument the practitioner trains in their own hands and attention, and each is coupled to the interventions of its own tradition. Those do not superimpose:

- A practitioner who can feel a lax tissue has not thereby learned to read the sequence of a five-phase disorder.
- A dosha reading built on digestion does not become a tissue-state reading because both of them name damp.

What lands on the page is an upper bound on how much two systems share. In practice they share less.

## Using it in teaching

- **Teach one system first.** Bring in the atlas afterwards, to show what that system cannot read, not as a way in.
- **Use the tray.** For any point that goes unassessed, ask what you would have to observe to read it, and in which system that observation is trained.
- **Push the conversation down to observation.** For a point that lands, ask what each tradition would touch, look at or ask about to place it there. That is where the systems part, and the page cannot go there for you.
- **Grade the declared system and the declared source, never a crosswalk.**
